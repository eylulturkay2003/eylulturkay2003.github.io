About

Following the biology from molecular mechanism to disease context.

My research interests focus on understanding how changes in protein activity and cellular regulation influence tumor growth, behavior, and responses to therapy.

During my MSc thesis, I worked with LIMD1, a tumor suppressor protein, and investigated which regions of LIMD1 may be involved in its interaction with the E3 ubiquitin ligase ARIH1. I used LIMD1 truncation constructs and carried out experiments involving recombinant protein expression, affinity purification, and pull down analysis. This work strengthened my interest in understanding how changes in individual protein interactions can influence the wider regulatory networks that shape tumor behavior.

I would like to develop this further by studying how specific molecular changes lead to particular cancer phenotypes. In the context of LIMD1, this means asking how loss of its function and disruption of associated signaling pathways may affect proliferation, response to therapy, interactions within the tumor microenvironment, and the ability of cancer cells to adapt when a pathway is disrupted. Treatment resistance is therefore a central research interest for me, particularly the molecular mechanisms that allow cancer cells to remain viable despite therapeutic pressure.

My interest in treatment resistance began during my BSc dissertation, where I studied pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma and the rationale for targeting two major energy producing pathways, glycolysis and mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation. The idea that blocking one pathway may cause tumor cells to rely more heavily on another source of ATP increased my interest in cancer cell adaptation and the mechanisms that allow tumors to survive under selective pressure.

Bioinformatics gave me the opportunity to approach related questions from a genomic perspective. I worked with open access sequencing datasets and used FastQC, Trimmomatic, and Geneious for reference mapping of reads to chromosome 17 of GRCh38, alongside coverage assessment and preliminary variant analysis. This experience showed me how strongly genomic interpretation depends on data quality and how limitations such as insufficient coverage can affect the conclusions that can be drawn.

My pathology experience also helped me place molecular findings within a wider disease context. Working in histopathology and molecular pathology environments gave me experience in understanding cancer through tissue morphology, staining patterns, and diagnostic characteristics. Previous exposure to FISH testing for BCL2, BCL6, MYC, and HER2 also helped me connect molecular alterations with their assessment in a diagnostic setting.

Together, these experiences have shaped my research interests in cancer signaling, tumor suppressor biology, therapy resistance, and molecular pathology. I am particularly interested in research that begins with a defined molecular question and follows that mechanism through to its effects on tumor behavior, adaptation, and response to therapy.
