BIOINFORMATICS TRAINING · RNA-SEQ

Public RNA-seq data and chromosome 17 mapping

A documented training workflow using public breast-cancer cell-line sequencing data to practice read quality control, trimming, reference mapping and coverage interpretation.

For viewing only — reproduction or redistribution is not permitted.

OVERVIEW

From public sequencing runs to mapped reads.

The internship report documents a bioinformatics exercise using two paired-end RNA-seq datasets obtained from the public NCBI Sequence Read Archive. The selected samples were breast-cancer cell lines. The stated downstream focus was chromosome 17, including practice interpreting BRCA1-region mapping and possible variants.

DATA & APPROACH

Quality control, trimming and reference mapping.

  1. Public data retrieval: locate sequencing runs in the NCBI SRA and retrieve paired-end FASTQ files through Galaxy using Fasterq-dump.
  2. Read quality: inspect per-base quality, GC content, duplication and overrepresented sequences in FastQC.
  3. Trimming: apply the documented Trimmomatic sliding-window quality filter (four-base window, minimum average Phred 20; discard reads shorter than 36 bases) and perform a second FastQC check.
  4. Reference: use the GRCh38.p14 chromosome 17 reference (NC_000017.11) and its annotations.
  5. Mapping: import trimmed paired-end reads into Geneious Prime and map them to the chromosome 17 reference; review mapped/unmapped reads and coverage statistics.

MY ROLE

Training analysis using public, non-patient-specific project data.

The report documents my work retrieving public sequencing runs, performing read-quality checks and trimming, preparing the reference, mapping reads, and reviewing alignment/coverage output in Geneious. This is computational training and analysis of public cell-line data; it is not clinical diagnostic work.

OBSERVATIONS & INTERPRETATION

Coverage determines what variant review can support.

The workflow report describes mapping to chromosome 17 to explore BRCA1 transcript abundance and variant detection. Its coverage was limited, and the source report contains internal inconsistencies. Consequently, mapped reads or candidate variation cannot be presented here as validated variants, reliable gene-expression measurements or biological conclusions.

The exercise demonstrates why read depth, uniformity, mapping quality and reference choice must be examined before interpreting a genomic locus. A chromosome-restricted mapping exercise also cannot characterize the whole transcriptome or genome.

LIMITATIONS & NEXT STEPS

Not a validated analysis pipeline.

The documented workflow uses a limited reference scope, and its record has inconsistencies and limited chromosome 17 coverage. It should not be represented as a validated end-to-end variant-calling pipeline. Stronger work would require a clearly versioned, reproducible workflow; broader reference coverage; documented read and mapping quality thresholds; sufficient locus-level depth; appropriate controls; and independent validation before interpreting candidate variants.

SKILLS DEMONSTRATED

Foundational sequencing-data handling.

  • Retrieving public SRA data and recording metadata.
  • FASTQ quality assessment with FastQC.
  • Quality-based trimming with Trimmomatic.
  • Working with GRCh38 reference sequences and chromosome annotations.
  • Read mapping and coverage review in Geneious Prime.
  • Recognizing the limits of coverage and avoiding unsupported variant conclusions.
